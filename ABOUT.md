# About this project and the person who built it

I am Ingrid Leiria, an economist and PhD candidate in Economics at Korea University Sejong, and a Chief of Staff in
a consulting firm. I build things. This is one of them.

The short version of how it started: a physician I collaborate with had a problem he had already tried to buy his
way out of. Patients were arriving through a messaging app at every hour, in numbers a front desk cannot answer
well, and every product he tested answered them in a way patients could feel was scripted. The word he used was
desumanizado. In a specialty where the person messaging you is considering changing something about their body
that they have been self conscious about for years, the moment the reply feels like a form, the conversation is
over.

I recognised that as a problem I could work on. Not because I had built anything like it before, but because the
hard part was not the software. The hard part was knowing what the right answer sounds like, and when there should
not be an answer at all.

## What I actually do on it

I write the specification and I read the conversations. Every week I go through what the assistant said, find the
places where it sounded like a machine or crossed a line it should not have crossed, and turn those observations
into instructions precise enough to change the behaviour. I decide what gets built and in what order. I test it by
using it. I design the escalation behaviour with the practice, which is the part that has actual consequences.

The code is written by AI tools under close supervision. That is not a disclaimer, it is the method, and the
[vibe coding story](VIBE_CODING_STORY.md) is about what that method really requires, which is more than people
assume and different from what they assume.

What I would say about my own contribution is this. The limiting factor in building software used to be technical
skill. It has moved. It is now precision about what you want and the discipline to reject anything that is not it,
and those are things I already did for a living.

## Why I do it without invoicing for it

I get asked this, so it is worth answering plainly rather than leaving it to be guessed at.

Three reasons, and none of them is charity.

**It is a research site.** Patient communication in small practices is barely studied, and this system generates
exactly the kind of data that question needs: how people actually decide about elective procedures, where they
stop, what they ask first, how long the process really takes. That work sits inside my field, health economics,
and I would not have access to it otherwise.

**It is the proof.** I wanted to know, in practice and not in theory, what it takes to put a system into production
with real people and real stakes using these tools. There is no honest way to find that out on a demo. The fact
that it has run against real patient conversations, in a regulated setting, is the entire result.

**It is a real relationship.** The practice trusted me with the first contact its patients have with it. That is
not a small thing to be handed, and the responsibility is what keeps the work going long past the point where a
side project usually stops.
